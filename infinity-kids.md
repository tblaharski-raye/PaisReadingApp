# STORY BRIEF: "The Infinity Kids"

Paste this whole document into a new chat. Everything below the brief is the finished book so far (Chapters 1–5), word for word. Keep writing from Chapter 6 in the same voice.

## The project
- A 20-chapter retelling of the Marvel Infinity Saga (Iron Man through Avengers: Endgame) for an elementary-age reader.
- One family of five original characters appears in EVERY chapter and helps the heroes in a real, story-changing way.
- Chapters 1–2 are about 1,500–1,800 words. Chapters 3–20 should be roughly 3,500–5,000 words each (Ch 3 ≈ 3,300, Ch 4 ≈ 3,700, Ch 5 ≈ 4,700). Each new chapter can run a little longer than the last.
- Deliver one chapter at a time as plain text that can be copied. Keep a running file of every approved chapter, saved word for word, so the full book can be compiled at the end.

## Writing style
- Elementary reading level: short sentences, simple words, lots of dialogue.
- Sound effects in caps (WHOOOSH, BOOM, CLANK, BEEP). Funny moments in every chapter.
- Heroes are warm and kind to the kids. Villains are sneaky or scary but never gory.
- Hard moments (losses, the snap, Natasha, Tony) are told gently and honestly: sad, but safe for a young reader.
- Retell the movie plots in original words. Don't copy long movie dialogue; short famous phrases are fine sparingly.
- Each girl should get her own moment to shine in most chapters, and the parents should do real things too.

## The family
- **Tim** (Dad). Works at Stark Tower. Steady, brave, a little clumsy (tomato on the head, face-first in pancake batter after Thor's back-slap). Is learning from Steve how to be a good "captain" for his family: never give up, nobody gets left behind. Wears his star pin on his shirt over his heart.
- **Lindsey** (Mom, Tim's wife). Always carries a map and never gets lost. "Best finder in the whole world." Calm, firm when needed ("the look"; taught Thor to say please). Writes key clues on her map. Pin is on her map.
- **Paisley** (daughter). Wears a sparkly crown everywhere; loves princesses, fairies, and puppies. Brave and gentle. Her signature move is the calming breath: in like smelling a flower, out like blowing out birthday candles, then hug a puppy. "Big feelings are easier with a friend." Thor calls her Princess Paisley. Taps her crown when she's thinking. Pin is on her crown.
- **Lainey** (daughter). Can't stand still; the fastest runner anyone has seen. Always tries to race things (Thor, Steve's shield) and "almost" keeps up. Nicknamed "the little rocket" / Thor calls her Little Rocket Lainey. Pin is on her shoe.
- **Sydney** (daughter). Carries a notebook of robot drawings; loves machines, math and clues. Notices what grown-ups miss. Thor calls her Hawk-Eyed Sydney. Pin is on her notebook.

# The Infinity Kids

## Chapter 1: The Iron Suit

Tim worked in a very tall building in New York City. It was so tall that the top of it poked into the clouds on foggy days. It was called Stark Tower.

Today was a special day. Tim was bringing his whole family to work with him.

There was his wife, Lindsey, who held a folded-up map in her hand. Lindsey liked to know exactly where she was and exactly where she was going. She never got lost, not even once.

And there were their three daughters.

There was Paisley, who wore a sparkly crown almost everywhere she went. She loved princesses, fairies, and puppies more than anything in the world.

There was Lainey, who could never stand still. She bounced. She hopped. She skipped. She was the fastest runner anyone had ever seen.

And there was Sydney, who carried a notebook full of drawings. Most of them were robots. Some of them were robots riding other robots.

"Okay, team," said Tim as they stood on the sidewalk. "Before we go in, I need everyone to promise one thing."

"What?" asked Lainey, hopping from one foot to the other.

"Stay close to Mom and me," said Tim. "This building is full of amazing things. But some of them are also a little bit dangerous."

"Dangerous how?" asked Sydney.

Lindsey smiled and winked at Tim. "You'll see."

The girls all promised. Then the family walked through the big glass doors.

Inside, the lobby was shiny and bright. The floor was so clean that Paisley could see her crown sparkling in it. There were tall plants, a fountain, and a giant screen on the wall that said WELCOME TO STARK INDUSTRIES.

"Good morning, Tim," said a calm voice out of nowhere.

Lainey spun around. "Who said that?"

"My name is Jarvis," said the voice. "I am the computer that helps run this building. Welcome, Lindsey. Welcome, young visitors."

Sydney's mouth fell open. "A computer that talks? That's the coolest thing I've ever heard!"

"Thank you," said Jarvis. "Mr. Stark is waiting for you upstairs. Please step into the elevator."

They piled into the elevator. Lindsey unfolded her map and pointed to a little square at the very top.

"We're going all the way up here," she said. "Floor ninety-three."

"Ninety-three!" said Lainey. "That's like a hundred!"

"Almost," said Lindsey, tapping Lainey on the nose.

The elevator zoomed up so fast that Paisley's tummy did a flip. She held onto her crown with one hand and her dad's hand with the other. Then the doors slid open with a soft DING.

The girls gasped.

They were standing in the biggest workshop they had ever seen. There were tools hanging on every wall. There were tables covered in wires and bolts and blinking lights. There were robot arms that moved all by themselves, picking things up and putting them down.

And there were sparks. Sparks were flying everywhere, like tiny orange fireworks.

"Whoa," whispered Sydney.

Then they heard a sound. WHOOOOSH!

Something red and gold zoomed over their heads! It flew in a big circle near the ceiling. Then it flew in another circle, even faster.

"It's a bird!" yelled Lainey.

"It's a plane!" yelled Paisley.

"It's a guy in a metal suit," said Lindsey, squinting up.

She was right. The red and gold shape swooped down and landed in front of them with a loud CLANK. It was a person wearing a suit made of shiny metal from head to toe. Even his face was covered by a metal mask with glowing eyes.

The mask flipped up with a click. Underneath was a man with dark hair, a neat little beard, and a big grin.

"Hi there," he said. "I'm Tony Stark. But most people call me Iron Man."

For a second, nobody said anything at all. Then all three girls started talking at once.

"You can FLY?"

"Is that suit heavy?"

"Can I try it?"

"Is there a bathroom in there?"

Tony laughed and held up his metal hands. "Whoa, whoa. One at a time. Tim, Lindsey, are these the helpers you told me about?"

"These are our girls," said Tim proudly. "Paisley, Lainey, and Sydney."

Tony looked at each of them. "Nice to meet you all. Now, who's the one who likes robots?"

Sydney raised her hand so fast she almost knocked her notebook over. "Me!"

"Let's see what you've got," said Tony.

Sydney opened her notebook and showed him her drawings. There was a robot that made pancakes. There was a robot that walked dogs. There was a robot with wings and wheels and a little umbrella on top.

Tony looked very closely at every page. He didn't rush. He even pointed at one drawing and nodded.

"These are good, kid," he said. "Really good. This one here, with the wings? You put the rockets on the bottom of the feet. That's exactly where I put mine."

Sydney turned pink. Lindsey gave her a proud squeeze on the shoulder.

Then Tony sighed and looked down at his boot. "But I've got a problem. My left boot rocket keeps sputtering. It goes WHOOSH, then PFFFT, then WHOOSH again. I almost crashed into the ceiling twice this morning."

"Can we help fix it?" asked Paisley.

Tony raised an eyebrow. "You want to help me fix the Iron Man suit?"

All three girls nodded. Tim and Lindsey nodded too.

Tony grinned. "Then grab some goggles. Safety first."

Everyone put on big clear goggles. Paisley put hers on right over her crown. Then they all got to work.

Sydney looked inside the boot with a tiny flashlight. Tim held the tools and handed them to Tony one at a time. Lindsey found the instruction book and read each step out loud in her clearest voice.

"Step one," she read. "Open the fuel panel."

Click. Tony opened it.

"Step two. Check the little blue wire."

Sydney peeked in. "It's loose! The blue wire is wiggly!"

"Good eye," said Tony. "Paisley, can you pass me a tiny screw?"

Paisley looked into the box of screws. There were hundreds of them. Big ones. Small ones. Silver ones. Gold ones. She picked the tiniest, shiniest one and held it up.

"This one?"

"Perfect," said Tony.

He tightened the blue wire with the tiny screw. Twist, twist, twist.

"Now we need to test it," said Tony. "Who wants to be the tester?"

"ME!" shouted Lainey.

Tony handed her a small remote with one big red button. "When I say go, you press this and run to the far wall. If the boot beeps the whole time, it's working. If it stops beeping, it's broken."

Lainey got into her running position. Tim and Lindsey looked at each other and smiled. They knew what was coming.

"Ready," said Tony. "Set. GO!"

Lainey pressed the button and took off like a rocket herself. Her shoes squeaked. Her hair flew behind her. She zoomed past the robot arms and around a table and touched the far wall.

BEEP. BEEP. BEEP. BEEP. The boot beeped the whole time!

"It works!" cheered Sydney.

"And that," said Tony, "was the fastest anybody has ever run in this room."

Lainey grinned so big her cheeks hurt.

Tony flipped his mask back down. His eyes glowed blue. "Stand back, everybody."

The family stepped back together. Tony bent his knees. Then, WHOOOOOSH! He shot straight up into the air. He did a loop. He did a spin. He flew higher than ever before, all the way up to the tall glass ceiling, and not one sputter.

He came back down and landed softly on his feet.

"Smooth as butter," he said, flipping up his mask. "You five make a great team."

"Five?" said Paisley, counting on her fingers. "There are only three of us girls."

Tony pointed at Tim and Lindsey. "I counted your mom and dad too. Best team I've had all week."

Everyone laughed. Then Tony did something surprising. He walked over to a drawer and pulled out five small metal pins. Each one was shaped like a tiny red and gold star.

"These are for you," he said, handing one to each of them. "Official Stark Tower helpers. If I ever need you, these pins will beep."

Paisley pinned hers right on her crown. Lainey pinned hers on her shoe. Sydney pinned hers on her notebook. Lindsey pinned hers on her map. Tim pinned his on his shirt, right over his heart.

On the drive home, the girls talked about Iron Man the whole time.

"Do you think he'll really call us?" asked Paisley from the back seat.

Tim looked at Lindsey. Lindsey looked out the window at Stark Tower shining in the evening sun.

"I think he might," she said.

They had no idea how right she was.

## Chapter 2: The Big Green Friend

Three days later, the family was eating pancakes for breakfast.

Paisley was cutting her pancake into a crown shape. Lainey was eating hers while doing jumping jacks. Sydney was drawing a pancake robot in her notebook. Lindsey was pouring more syrup, and Tim was reading the newspaper.

Then something went BEEP.

Everyone stopped. BEEP. BEEP. BEEP.

"It's the pins!" shouted Sydney.

Sure enough, all five star pins were blinking red and gold. Paisley's was blinking on her crown. Lainey's was blinking on her shoe. Sydney's was blinking on her notebook. Lindsey's was blinking on her map, and Tim's was blinking on his shirt.

Tim pressed the middle of his pin. A tiny picture of Tony Stark popped up in the air, glowing blue.

"Morning, team," said Tony. "Sorry to interrupt breakfast. Is that syrup on your chin, Lainey?"

Lainey wiped her chin very fast.

"What do you need, Tony?" asked Lindsey.

"I need help finding a friend of mine," said Tony. "His name is Bruce Banner. He's a scientist. A really smart one. Maybe the smartest person I know, besides me."

"Where is he?" asked Tim.

"That's the problem," said Tony. "Bruce likes to hide. He's somewhere in a big lab building across the city, but it has about a hundred rooms. I need someone good at finding things."

Everyone looked at Lindsey. Lindsey was already unfolding her map.

"We're on our way," she said.

The family finished their pancakes in record time. Lainey finished hers in about four seconds.

The lab building was big and gray and very quiet. The hallways were long, and every door looked the same.

"How are we ever going to find him?" asked Paisley.

Lindsey looked at her map. Then she looked down the hallway. Then she looked at the map again.

"If I were a scientist who liked to hide," she said, "I'd pick the quietest room. One far away from everyone else." She pointed. "This one. At the very end. Room 99."

They tiptoed down the hall. Lainey had to try very hard not to run. At the end of the hall, there was a door with a little sign on it. The sign said: QUIET PLEASE.

Tim knocked gently. Knock, knock.

"Come in," said a soft voice.

Inside was a small, tidy lab. There were bubbling beakers, stacks of books, and a little plant on the windowsill. A man sat at a desk. He had messy gray hair, glasses, and a kind face. He wore a purple shirt with the sleeves rolled up.

"Hello," he said. He looked a little nervous. "Tony sent you, didn't he?"

"He did," said Tim. "I'm Tim. This is my wife, Lindsey, and our girls, Paisley, Lainey, and Sydney."

"I'm Bruce," said the man. He gave a small wave. "Nice to meet you. How did you find me? Nobody ever finds me."

"My mom found you," said Sydney proudly. "She's the best finder in the whole world."

Lindsey smiled. "I just picked the quietest room."

Bruce laughed a little. "That's exactly why I picked it."

Sydney walked over to the bubbling beakers. "What are you making?"

"Medicine," said Bruce. "I'm trying to help people feel better."

"That's nice," said Paisley. "Why do you like to hide, though? Are you shy?"

Bruce took off his glasses and rubbed them on his shirt. He was quiet for a long moment.

"I have a secret," he said finally. "It's a big one. Can I tell you?"

All three girls nodded. Tim and Lindsey sat down on a bench to listen.

"A long time ago," said Bruce, "I had an accident in my lab. Ever since then, when I get very angry or very scared, something happens. I turn green. I grow really, really big. I get super strong. And I turn into somebody else. A giant green guy called the Hulk."

Lainey stopped bouncing for the first time all day. "A giant GREEN guy?"

"Is he scary?" asked Sydney in a small voice.

"He can be," said Bruce. "He gets loud, and he smashes things. That's why I hide. I don't want anybody to get hurt."

Paisley thought about that for a long time. She tapped her crown, which is what she did when she was thinking hard.

"Is the Hulk a bad guy?" she asked.

"No," said Bruce. "I don't think so. He just has really big feelings. And he doesn't know what to do with them."

"Oh," said Paisley. "I have big feelings too sometimes. When Lainey takes my markers without asking, I get SO mad."

"Hey!" said Lainey. Then she said, "Okay, that's true."

"So what do you do," asked Bruce, "when you get that mad?"

Paisley sat up very straight. "Mom taught me. I take deep breaths. In through my nose like I'm smelling a flower. Out through my mouth like I'm blowing out birthday candles. And then I go hug my puppy."

Lindsey smiled. Bruce smiled too.

"That's a really good idea," he said. "Maybe I should try that."

Just then, the little plant on the windowsill tipped over. It bumped into a tall glass beaker. The beaker wobbled. It wobbled again. And then—

SMASH!

It crashed onto the floor and broke into a hundred pieces. Green goo splashed everywhere.

Bruce jumped up from his chair. His eyes went wide. His hands started to shake.

"Oh no," he said. "Oh no, oh no. Everybody get back."

His skin started turning green. First his hands. Then his arms. Then his face. His purple shirt stretched tighter, and tighter, and RRRRIP! He grew bigger, and BIGGER, and BIGGER, until his head bumped the ceiling.

Standing in the tiny lab was a giant green man with huge muscles and an angry face.

"HULK SMASH!" he roared. The windows rattled. The books shook on the shelves.

Tim stood in front of the girls. Lindsey grabbed Lainey and Sydney's hands. Everyone was frozen.

Everyone except Paisley.

Paisley took one small step forward. Her knees were shaking a little. But she straightened her crown, and she took another step.

"Paisley," whispered Tim. "Careful."

"It's okay, Daddy," she whispered back. "He just has big feelings."

The Hulk looked down at her. He was so tall, and she was so small.

"Hulk," Paisley said in her calmest voice. "It's okay. It was just an accident. Can you breathe with me? Like this."

She took a big breath in through her nose. Like smelling a flower.

The Hulk stared at her. His nose wrinkled. Then, very slowly, he copied her. He took a giant breath in. SNIIIIFF.

"Good job!" said Paisley. "Now out, like birthday candles."

She blew out slowly. The Hulk blew out too. WHOOOOOSH. It was so strong it blew Lindsey's map right out of her hands.

"In," said Paisley. The Hulk breathed in.

"Out," said Paisley. The Hulk breathed out.

Now Lainey and Sydney started breathing too. Then Lindsey. Then Tim. The whole family stood in the lab, breathing in and out together with a giant green Hulk.

In... out... In... out...

Slowly, the Hulk started to shrink. His big green hands got smaller. His green face turned pink again. He got smaller, and smaller, until it was just Bruce, sitting on the floor in a very ripped purple shirt.

He blinked. He looked around. He looked at Paisley.

"Wow," Bruce whispered. "Nobody has ever done that before. Nobody has ever helped him calm down."

Paisley sat down next to him. "You just needed a friend," she said. "Big feelings are easier with a friend."

Then she gave him a hug. Bruce hugged her back.

Tim let out a long breath. He knelt down next to Paisley and put his hand on her shoulder. "That," he said, "was the bravest thing I have ever seen."

Lindsey picked up her map and wiped her eyes. "We are so proud of you, sweetheart."

"Can you guys help me clean up the green goo?" asked Bruce with a shy smile.

"Yes!" said Sydney. "I'll get the mop!"

"I'll get it faster!" said Lainey, and she zoomed out the door.

When the lab was clean, Bruce found a new shirt. It was a little too small, which made everyone giggle.

"Tony needs your help on a big project," Tim told him. "Will you come with us?"

Bruce looked at the family. He looked at Paisley.

"If you're all going to be there," he said, "then yes. I'll come."

On the way out, Paisley held Bruce's hand.

"Next time you get big feelings," she said, "I'll bring my puppy. He's really good at hugs."

Bruce laughed. "It's a deal."

## Chapter 3: The Hammer from the Sky

It was the first warm night of summer, and the family was having a campout in the backyard.

Tim had set up the big green tent under the oak tree. Lindsey had brought out sleeping bags, pillows, flashlights, and a giant bag of marshmallows. The girls had brought out almost everything else they owned.

Paisley brought her crown, three stuffed puppies, and a fairy nightlight. Lainey brought a soccer ball, a jump rope, and a frisbee, even though it was almost bedtime. Sydney brought her notebook and a small telescope that she had built herself out of a paper towel tube and two magnifying glasses.

"Does it actually work?" asked Lainey, poking the telescope.

"Kind of," said Sydney. "Everything looks bigger and a little bit blurry."

"That's how everything looks when I'm tired," said Tim.

They sat around the little fire pit and roasted marshmallows. Paisley liked hers golden brown. Lainey liked hers on fire. Sydney liked hers exactly halfway between golden and on fire, and she wrote down how many seconds it took.

Lindsey unfolded a special map. It wasn't a map of streets or buildings. It was a map of the stars.

"See that bright one?" she said, pointing up. "That's the North Star. If you ever get lost at night, you can find it, and it will help you find your way home."

"You know maps of the SKY too?" asked Paisley.

"I know maps of everything," said Lindsey with a smile.

The stars were twinkling. The fire was crackling. The crickets were chirping. Everything was calm and cozy.

Then, far away, there was a rumble.

Rrrrrrrrumble.

Everyone looked up. A dark cloud was rolling across the sky. It covered up the North Star. Then it covered up the moon.

"That's strange," said Lindsey, checking her star map. "There wasn't supposed to be any rain tonight."

The wind started to blow. The tent flapped. The fire flickered. Paisley grabbed her three stuffed puppies.

"Maybe we should go inside," said Tim.

But before anyone could move, the sky lit up bright white.

FLASH!

A giant bolt of lightning came straight down out of the cloud. It didn't hit a tree. It didn't hit the house. It hit the garden, right in the middle of Lindsey's tomato plants.

BOOOOOOM!

The ground shook. Dirt flew up into the air. Leaves and tomatoes rained down everywhere. One tomato landed right on top of Tim's head with a SPLAT.

Then everything was quiet.

The dark cloud rolled away as fast as it had come. The stars came back out. The moon came back out. The crickets started chirping again, a little nervously.

Tim wiped the tomato off his head. "Is everybody okay?"

"I'm okay," said Paisley.

"I'm okay," said Sydney.

"That was AWESOME," said Lainey.

Lindsey was staring at her garden. Where her tomato plants used to be, there was now a big round hole in the ground. It was like a giant bowl. Smoke was rising out of it.

And someone was climbing out.

First, they saw a big hand grab the edge of the hole. Then a second hand. Then a head with long golden hair. Then a pair of very wide shoulders with a red cape flapping behind them.

A very tall man stood up in the middle of the tomato crater. He wore shiny silver armor. In one hand, he held a hammer. It was square and gray, with a short handle wrapped in leather.

He looked around the backyard. He looked at the tent. He looked at the fire pit. He looked at the family, who were all standing very still.

Then he smiled a huge, friendly smile.

"Greetings!" he said in a voice so big it echoed off the house. "I am Thor, son of Odin, Prince of Asgard!"

For a moment, nobody said anything.

Then Paisley stepped forward and curtsied, the way princesses do in her storybooks.

"Hello, Prince Thor," she said. "I'm Princess Paisley. Well, not a real princess. But I have a crown."

Thor bowed back to her very seriously. "It is a fine crown, Princess Paisley."

Paisley beamed.

"I'm Tim," said Tim, stepping forward. "This is my wife, Lindsey. And these are our daughters, Paisley, Lainey, and Sydney."

"And that," said Lindsey, pointing at the crater, "was my tomato garden."

Thor looked down at the crater. He looked at the squashed tomatoes. He looked a little embarrassed.

"Ah," he said. "My apologies, Lady Lindsey. Landing is not my strongest skill."

"Where did you come from?" asked Sydney. She held up her paper towel telescope and looked at him through it. Thor looked bigger and a little blurry.

"From Asgard," said Thor. He pointed up at the sky. "It is a kingdom far beyond your stars."

Lindsey unfolded her star map. She looked at it carefully. She turned it sideways. She turned it upside down.

"Asgard," she said. "That's not on here."

"It would not be," said Thor. "It is very, very far. To get there, you must travel across the Bifrost. It is a bridge made of rainbow light."

"A RAINBOW bridge?" gasped Paisley. "Like in a fairy tale?"

"Better than a fairy tale," said Thor. "It shimmers with every color. When you step on it, it sings."

"What is Asgard like?" asked Sydney, getting out her notebook.

Thor sat down on a log by the fire pit. The log creaked under him. "Asgard is a golden city. The towers are so tall they touch the clouds. There are great halls with feasts every night. There are gardens with golden apples. And my father, Odin, is the king."

"Do you have any pets?" asked Paisley.

Thor grinned. "My father has a horse. His name is Sleipnir. He has eight legs."

Paisley's mouth fell open. "EIGHT legs?"

"Eight," said Thor. "He is the fastest horse in all the worlds."

Lainey crossed her arms. "I bet I could beat him."

Thor laughed so loud that a bird flew out of the oak tree. "I like this one! She has the heart of a warrior."

Just then, Thor's tummy made a very loud noise.

GRRRRRRROOOOOWL.

Everyone stared at his tummy.

"Forgive me," said Thor. "Traveling across the Bifrost makes one very hungry."

Lindsey smiled. "Come inside. I think we have some leftover pancakes."

Everyone went into the kitchen. Thor had to duck to fit through the back door. He still bumped his head. He sat down at the kitchen table, and the chair creaked and groaned under him.

Thor laid his hammer down on the table. The table creaked and groaned too.

Lindsey warmed up a big stack of pancakes and poured Thor a tall mug of milk. Thor ate the first pancake in one bite. Then the second. Then the third. He drank the whole mug of milk in one big gulp.

Then he did something very surprising. He held the empty mug high in the air and shouted, "ANOTHER!"

And he threw the mug down on the floor.

CRASH!

The mug broke into pieces. The girls gasped. Tim's eyebrows went all the way up.

Lindsey put her hands on her hips. She gave Thor the look. It was the look she gave the girls when they did something they knew they weren't supposed to do.

"Thor," she said calmly. "In this house, we don't throw mugs. When we want more, we say please."

Thor looked down at the broken mug. Then he looked at Lindsey. Then he looked at the three girls, who were all staring at him.

His cheeks turned pink.

Nick Fury looked right at them through the little blue picture. "Especially the girls. Tony says they notice things nobody else notices. Right now, I need that."

The picture blinked out.

Tim and Lindsey looked at each other. Then they looked at their three daughters, standing in their pajamas, looking back at them with very big eyes.

"Okay," said Lindsey. "Here's the plan. Everybody gets dressed. Everybody brushes teeth. And everybody stays with Mom and Dad no matter what. Deal?"

"Deal," said three voices.

"Lainey," said Tim. "Your shoes are on the wrong feet."

Lainey looked down. "Oh."

Ten minutes later, a big black car was waiting outside. It drove them through the dark, quiet city to an empty field. And in the middle of the field was the biggest thing any of them had ever seen.

It was a ship. A giant, gray, metal ship, as big as a whole town. It had runways on top for airplanes. It had towers and lights and antennas.

"Is that a boat?" asked Paisley. "Why is there a boat in a field?"

"Wait for it," said a voice behind them.

They turned around. A woman with short red hair was walking toward them. She wore a black suit and had a calm, clever face. She moved very quietly. Lainey hadn't even heard her coming.

"I'm Natasha," she said. "Some people call me Black Widow. I'm a spy."

"A real spy?" whispered Sydney.

"A real spy," said Natasha. "Now watch."

The giant ship began to rumble. Big fans on its sides started to spin. Slowly, slowly, the whole enormous ship lifted off the ground and rose up into the sky.

"It FLIES?" shouted Lainey.

"It's called the Helicarrier," said Natasha. "It's Nick Fury's flying headquarters. Come on. Let's get you on board before it leaves without you."

A little plane flew them up and landed on the Helicarrier's runway. When they climbed out, the wind was so strong that Paisley had to hold her crown with both hands. Up here, above the clouds, the sun was just starting to rise. Everything was pink and gold.

Natasha led them inside to a big round room full of computers and screens. Nick Fury stood in the middle. Tony was there. Bruce was there. Steve was there, with his shield on his back.

And sticking out of Steve's jacket pocket was one little brown floppy ear.

"You brought Biscuit!" said Paisley.

Steve smiled and patted his pocket. "He's a good luck charm."

"Where's Thor?" asked Tim.

"Out looking for his brother," said Tony. "He'll turn up. He always does. Usually with a lot of thunder."

Nick Fury pointed at the big screen. It showed a map of the world with little red dots blinking on it.

"Loki has the Tesseract," he said. "But he can't just use it right away. He needs to make a machine to open a huge door in the sky. A door big enough for an army to come through."

"An army?" said Bruce. "From where?"

"From space," said Nick Fury. "Some very scary aliens have promised to help Loki rule the Earth. All he has to do is open the door for them."

The room went very quiet.

Then Thor came in through the door with a big BOOM of thunder, just like Tony said. He was holding his hammer in one hand. And with his other hand, he was dragging someone along by the arm.

The someone was tall and thin, with long black hair slicked back. He wore green and gold clothes and a golden helmet with two big curly horns on top. He did not look happy.

"I found him!" said Thor proudly. "He was in Germany, giving a speech."

"Hello, brother," said Loki in a smooth, sneaky voice. "You didn't have to pull so hard."

"He has horns!" whispered Paisley. "Like a goat!"

Loki turned and glared at her. Paisley stepped behind Lindsey's leg. But she peeked out anyway.

"We caught him a little too easily," said Steve, frowning. "That makes me nervous."

"Me too," said Natasha.

They put Loki inside a special room with glass walls. It was like a big round fishbowl, right in the middle of the Helicarrier. Loki walked around in a slow circle inside it, smiling like he knew a secret.

Lindsey watched him very carefully. Then she took out her map and wrote under LOKI = TROUBLE:

He wanted to get caught. Why?

"Mommy," whispered Sydney. "Where's his scepter? The stick with the blue jewel?"

"Tony's team took it," said Lindsey. "It's in the science lab."

"Can we go look at it?" asked Sydney. "Something about that jewel is bugging me."

So Bruce and Tony took the family down to the lab. The scepter was lying on a metal table, under bright lights. The blue jewel at the top glowed softly, like it was breathing.

Sydney walked around the table slowly, writing in her notebook. Bruce and Tony started looking at the scepter with machines that beeped and buzzed.

But after a few minutes, something strange started happening.

"Tony, you're holding that wrong," said Bruce.

"I'm holding it fine," said Tony. "You're the one who's doing it wrong."

Steve came into the lab. "What's going on in here?"

"Nobody asked you, Captain Old-Timey," snapped Tony.

"Well, somebody has to keep you in line," Steve snapped back.

Then Thor came in. Then Natasha. Then Nick Fury. And soon everyone was arguing. Tony was arguing with Steve. Steve was arguing with Nick Fury. Thor was arguing with everyone, very loudly. Bruce was rubbing his head and breathing faster and faster.

Even Tim and Lindsey started to argue.

"We should take the girls home," said Tim.

"We promised to help," said Lindsey.

"It's too dangerous," said Tim.

"You're not the boss of everything," said Lindsey.

Paisley covered her ears. Lainey looked back and forth like she was watching a tennis match. Everyone was getting louder and louder and grumpier and grumpier.

Then Sydney noticed something.

Every time someone yelled, the blue jewel on the scepter glowed a little brighter.

She looked at the jewel. She looked at the grown-ups. She looked at the jewel again.

Then she climbed up on a chair, took a giant breath, and yelled as loud as she could:

"STOP! IT'S THE STICK!"

Everyone stopped. Everyone turned and looked at her.

"It's the stick," said Sydney again, pointing at the scepter. "Every time you guys get mad, the jewel gets brighter. It's making you grumpy! It's making you fight!"

Everyone looked at the scepter. The blue jewel flickered, like it was surprised it got caught.

"She's right," said Bruce slowly. "That's what Loki wanted. He wanted us fighting each other instead of fighting him."

Tony rubbed his face. "Huh. That's sneaky."

Tim looked at Lindsey. Lindsey looked at Tim.

"Sorry," they both said at the same time. Then they laughed and hugged.

Paisley let go of her ears. "Big feelings are easier with a friend," she said. "Not with a grumpy stick."

Thor put a big hand on Sydney's shoulder. "Little one, you have the eyes of a hawk."

"Speaking of hawks," said Natasha, suddenly looking worried. "Where's Clint?"

BOOOOM!

The whole Helicarrier shook. The lights went red. Alarms started blaring everywhere.

"We're under attack!" said Nick Fury. "It's Hawkeye! He's leading Loki's helpers! They've blown up one of the engines!"

The floor tilted. Everyone grabbed onto something. The giant flying ship was starting to sink.

"If we lose another engine, this whole ship falls out of the sky," said Nick Fury.

Tony was already running. "I can fix the engine from the outside. But someone has to pull a lever on the inside at exactly the right time."

"I'll do it," said Steve.

"I'll help!" said Sydney. "I'm good at reading machine stuff!"

Tim looked at Lindsey. Lindsey nodded. "Go with them," she said to Tim. "Stay with Sydney. I'll keep Paisley and Lainey with me."

Tim, Sydney, and Steve ran to the engine room. Tony flew outside in his Iron Man suit. The engine was smoking. Big metal pieces were bent and broken.

Inside, there was a wall full of buttons and switches and one big red lever. Sydney looked at the panel. There were lots of numbers on a screen, and they were blinking.

"Tony!" she said into Tim's star pin. "The number on the screen says the engine is spinning too slow! It needs to get faster!"

"I'm pushing it from out here," said Tony's voice. "Tell me when it hits the green line."

Sydney watched the numbers climb. Higher. Higher. Steve grabbed the big red lever. Tim held onto Sydney so she wouldn't slip on the tilted floor.

"Almost," said Sydney. "Almost... almost..."

The number hit the green line.

"NOW!" shouted Sydney.

Steve pulled the lever with all his strength. CLUNK!

The engine roared back to life. The fans spun. The floor tilted back to flat. The Helicarrier stopped sinking.

Tony flew back in, his suit covered in black smoke. "Nice timing, kid."

Sydney grinned so big her cheeks hurt.

Meanwhile, Natasha had found Clint. He was still under Loki's spell. His eyes were glowing blue, and he was very hard to catch.

But Natasha was the best spy in the world. She did a flip. She did a spin. She dodged every arrow. And finally, she gave Clint a big BONK on the side of his head.

Clint fell down. He blinked. He shook his head.

His eyes went from glowing blue back to regular brown.

"Natasha?" he said, rubbing his head. "What happened? My head hurts."

"You've been working for the bad guy," said Natasha. "Welcome back."

"Ugh," said Clint. "That's embarrassing."

But while everyone was busy saving the ship, something else was happening. When Lindsey brought Paisley and Lainey back to the big round room, the glass cell in the middle was empty.

Loki was gone.

And so was the scepter.

"No, no, no," said Nick Fury, slamming his fist on a table.

Everyone gathered back together in the big round room. They were tired. They were scratched up. Bruce had turned into the Hulk during the explosion and fallen off the ship, and he had just come back, wearing a pair of borrowed pants that were much too big for him.

Paisley ran up and hugged him. "Are you okay?"

"I'm okay," said Bruce. "I landed in a barn. A very surprised farmer gave me these pants."

Everybody laughed a little. But not for long.

"So where did Loki go?" asked Steve.

"He needs to build his machine to open the door in the sky," said Thor. "He needs the Tesseract. And he needs a LOT of power."

"Where would he find that much power?" asked Natasha.

Lindsey unfolded her map of the city. She looked at it for a long time. She looked at all the buildings. Then she slowly put her finger on one of them.

The tallest one. The shiniest one. The one that was powered by its own special, super-strong energy.

"Tony," she said. "Your tower runs on its own power, right? Clean energy. Enough for the whole building, all by itself?"

Tony's face went pale. "Stark Tower. He's going to use my tower."

"He wants everyone to see," said Steve. "He wants to show off."

"That sounds like my brother," said Thor.

Nick Fury looked at all of them. Tony. Steve. Thor. Bruce. Natasha. Clint. Tim and Lindsey. Paisley, Lainey, and Sydney.

"Then let's go," he said.

By the time they flew to New York, it was already happening.

A bright blue beam of light was shooting up from the top of Stark Tower into the sky. And where the beam hit the clouds, a giant hole had opened up. It was round and black, and you could see stars inside it, even though it was the middle of the day.

And out of the hole came the aliens.

They were gray and bumpy, with glowing eyes. They rode on flying metal scooters that zoomed and swooped through the air. There were hundreds of them.

Then something even bigger came through the hole. It was like a giant flying whale made of metal and armor. It was as long as ten school buses. It swam through the sky between the buildings, and when its tail hit a building, the windows went CRASH.

People on the streets screamed and ran.

The family stood on the sidewalk with the heroes, staring up.

"Okay," said Lainey in a very small voice. "That's scary."

Tim knelt down and gathered all three girls in a big hug. Lindsey knelt down too.

"Listen to me," said Tim. "Being scared is okay. Everybody here is scared. Even the heroes. But being brave means doing the right thing even when you're scared."

"Like Steve standing up to the bullies," said Paisley.

"Exactly like that," said Lindsey.

Steve stepped up. He looked at everyone gathered around him. And he started giving orders, calm and clear, like a real captain.

"Tony, you take the sky. Stop as many of those scooters as you can. Clint, get up high on a rooftop. You're our eyes. Tell us where they're going. Thor, you can call lightning. Hit that hole and slow them down. Natasha and I will stay on the ground and protect people."

Then Steve turned to Tim and Lindsey.

"And you," he said. "Your family has the most important job of all. Get the people out. Get every single person somewhere safe. Nobody gets left behind."

Tim stood up a little straighter. "Nobody gets left behind," he said.

Then Steve looked around. "We're missing someone."

Just then, a little motorcycle came puttering down the street. It was old and rattly and very slow. On top of it sat Bruce Banner.

"Sorry I'm late," he said. "Traffic."

Paisley ran up to him. "Bruce! Are you going to be the Hulk?"

Bruce smiled at her. "You know how you taught me to calm down when the big feelings come?"

"Yeah?"

"Well, today," said Bruce, "I'm going to let them out on purpose. For something good."

He turned around and walked toward the giant metal whale, which was swimming straight down the street toward them.

His skin turned green. He got bigger. And BIGGER. And BIGGER.

The giant whale opened its mouth.

The Hulk pulled back his huge green fist.

And he punched it right on the nose.

WHAMMMM!

The whole giant whale stopped. Then it flipped over on its back, crashed into the street, and didn't get up.

"YEAH, HULK!" cheered Paisley, jumping up and down. "USE YOUR BIG FEELINGS!"

The Hulk turned around, looked at Paisley, and gave her a big green thumbs up.

All six of them stood together in a circle in the middle of the street. Iron Man. Captain America. Thor. Hulk. Black Widow. Hawkeye. Back to back, facing outward. The aliens circled above them.

Steve held up his shield.

"Avengers," he said. "Assemble!"

And they went to work.

So did the family.

Lindsey unfolded her map and studied it fast. "The aliens are coming from the top of the tower and flying toward the river," she said. "So the safest place is away from the river. Underground. The subway station on Forty-Second Street is big and deep. We'll send everyone there."

"How do we get the people there?" asked Tim.

"I've got it!" said Lainey. She took off running.

Lainey ran down the street faster than she had ever run before. She ran into a coffee shop. "Everybody follow me! Subway station! This way!" She ran into a bookstore. "Subway station! Go, go, go!" She ran into a toy store. "Subway! Now!"

People started following her. They followed her down the street and around the corner. Lindsey stood at the corner pointing the way with her map. "That way! Down the stairs! Keep going!"

Lainey zoomed back and found more people. And more. And more. She ran up and down the streets so fast that people started calling her "the little rocket."

Sydney found a traffic light that was broken and stuck on red. Cars were all jammed up, honking, and nobody could move. She opened the little metal box at the bottom of the light pole. Inside was a mess of wires.

"Daddy, hold the flashlight," she said.

Tim held the flashlight. Sydney found the loose wire, just like the one in Tony's boot. She tightened it. The light turned green. The cars started moving and got away from the danger.

Then Paisley heard something.

Under all the noise, under all the crashing and zooming and shouting, there was a tiny sound. Somebody was crying.

She looked around. There, hiding behind a mailbox, was a little boy. He was even younger than she was. He had his knees pulled up to his chin, and he was crying so hard he couldn't talk.

Paisley walked over and sat down next to him.

"Hi," she said. "I'm Paisley. What's your name?"

"M-M-Max," said the boy. "I lost my mom."

"That's really scary," said Paisley. "I'd be scared too."

Max nodded and cried some more.

"Want to know a trick?" said Paisley. "Breathe in, like you're smelling a flower."

Max sniffed.

"Now out, like birthday candles."

Max blew out. Then he did it again. And again. And slowly, he stopped crying.

"Good job," said Paisley. "Now let's go find your mom. My mom is really good at finding things."

She took Max's hand and walked him to Lindsey. Lindsey picked Max up and held him on her hip. "What does your mom look like, sweetie?"

"She has a yellow coat," said Max.

Lindsey looked down the crowded street. She looked up. She looked left. She looked right. Then she pointed.

There, way down at the bottom of the subway stairs, was a lady in a yellow coat. She was shouting, "MAX! MAX! Where are you?"

"MOM!" yelled Max.

Lindsey hurried down the stairs and handed Max to his mom. His mom hugged him so tight his feet came off the ground.

"Thank you," his mom said, crying happy tears. "Thank you so much."

"It was my daughter who found him," said Lindsey, pointing at Paisley.

Paisley straightened her crown and smiled.

Up in the sky, the Avengers were fighting hard. Tony zoomed between buildings with aliens chasing him. Thor called down lightning that crackled across the sky. Clint stood on a rooftop and shot arrows that never, ever missed. Natasha and Steve protected a whole bus full of people. And the Hulk jumped from building to building, smashing the alien scooters out of the air.

But no matter how many aliens they stopped, more kept coming out of the hole in the sky.

"We have to close that door," said Steve.

"The machine on top of the tower is protected by a force field," said Tony. "I can't break it. Nothing can."

Sydney was standing next to Tim, listening through his star pin. She flipped open her notebook. She looked at the page where she wrote SCEPTER. BLUE JEWEL. BAD.

"Daddy," she said slowly. "Loki's scepter has a jewel in it. And the Tesseract has a jewel in it. What if they're the same kind of thing? What if the scepter can poke through the force field?"

Tim pressed his pin. "Natasha! Sydney thinks the scepter can get through the force field!"

"Where is the scepter?" asked Natasha.

Clint's voice came on from the rooftop. "Loki dropped it. It's on the top of Stark Tower. Right next to the machine."

Natasha jumped on one of the alien scooters and flew it straight up to the top of Stark Tower. She grabbed the scepter. She pushed the tip of it against the force field.

The scepter's jewel touched the force field. Crackle. Fizzle. It went right through!

"She was right!" said Natasha. "I can close it!"

But just then, Nick Fury's voice came on, and it sounded scared.

"Everyone, listen. Some very worried people far away have sent a giant rocket toward New York. They think it's the only way to stop the aliens. But it would hurt the whole city. It'll be here in three minutes."

Everyone froze.

"I've got it," said Tony.

He flew up, up, up into the sky. He found the giant rocket zooming toward the city. He grabbed it with both arms. And instead of letting it hit the city, he turned around and carried it straight up toward the hole in the sky.

"Tony, that's a one-way trip," said Steve quietly.

"Save the rest for the ride home," said Tony.

He flew up through the hole. Into the dark, starry space on the other side. He let go of the rocket. And the rocket flew far, far away, right at the aliens' giant spaceship.

KA-BOOOOOM!

A huge flash of light lit up space. Every single alien on Earth suddenly stopped. Their scooters fell out of the sky. The giant whales stopped swimming. It was like someone had turned off all of them at once.

"Close it!" said Steve.

Natasha pushed the scepter hard against the machine. The blue beam flickered. The hole in the sky started to shrink.

But Tony hadn't come back yet.

"Come on, Tony," whispered Sydney, holding Tim's hand tight. "Come on."

The hole got smaller. And smaller.

And just before it closed, a tiny red and gold shape came falling out of it.

It was Tony. His suit was dark. His glowing eyes were off. He was falling. Falling fast. He wasn't flying anymore.

"He's not slowing down," said Thor, starting to spin his hammer.

But before Thor could even take off, a giant green shape leaped up off the side of a building. The Hulk jumped higher than he had ever jumped before. He caught Tony in his huge green arms. Then he slid all the way down the side of a building and landed on the street with a THUD.

He gently laid Tony on the ground.

Everyone ran over. Steve. Thor. The family. They all crowded around.

Tony wasn't moving.

Paisley grabbed Lindsey's hand. Lainey grabbed Tim's hand. Sydney's eyes filled up with tears.

Then the Hulk leaned down really close to Tony's face. And he ROARED.

"RAAAAAAAAAAAAR!"

Tony's eyes popped wide open. He gasped. He blinked.

"What just happened?" he said. "Please tell me nobody kissed me."

Everyone burst out laughing and cheering. Sydney threw her arms around him. "You're okay!"

"I'm okay," said Tony, hugging her back. "Did we win?"

"We won," said Steve.

Tony lay back on the street and looked up at the blue sky. The hole was gone. "Great. Hooray. Okay. Has anybody ever tried shawarma? There's a shawarma place two blocks from here. I've never had it. I want to try it."

"What's shawarma?" asked Lainey.

"I don't know," said Tony. "That's why I want to try it."

"We're not done yet," said Thor.

They all marched to the top of Stark Tower. And there, lying in a Loki-shaped dent in the floor, was Loki. The Hulk had found him first. He had picked Loki up by his ankle and bonked him on the floor, back and forth, like a floppy rag doll.

"Puny god," the Hulk had grumbled.

Loki groaned. He looked up at all the heroes standing around him. Then he looked at the family.

"I think," said Loki weakly, "I'll have a drink now."

"I think you won't," said Lindsey, putting her hands on her hips.

Thor took the Tesseract out of the machine. It glowed softly in his hands, blue and bright.

"What is it, really?" asked Sydney. "What's inside it?"

"Inside the cube is something very old and very powerful," said Thor. "It is called the Space Stone. It can make doorways to anywhere in the whole universe. It is one of six special stones. They are called the Infinity Stones. Each one has a different power."

"Six?" said Lindsey. She unfolded her map and turned it over to the empty back side. "Then we'd better keep track."

At the top, she wrote:

THE INFINITY STONES

And underneath it:

1. Space Stone (blue). Inside the Tesseract. Thor is taking it to Asgard.

Sydney looked over at the scepter, which Natasha was holding very carefully. The jewel at the top was still glowing.

"Is that one too?" asked Sydney.

Thor looked at the scepter for a long time. "I do not know," he said. "But it might be."

Lindsey wrote a little question mark at the bottom of her list.

Later that afternoon, Thor took Loki and the Tesseract home to Asgard across the rainbow bridge. Before he left, he bowed to each of the girls.

"Princess Paisley. Little Rocket Lainey. Hawk-Eyed Sydney," he said. "You were very brave today."

Then he shook Tim's hand. Very gently this time. Tim did not fall into anything.

And then everyone went to get shawarma.

The shawarma place was small, and it had gotten a little bit smashed during the battle. Some of the chairs were tipped over. One wall had a big crack in it. But the owner was so happy to see the Avengers that he cooked for all of them anyway.

They sat around a long table. Tony. Steve. Bruce. Natasha. Clint. Tim and Lindsey. Paisley, Lainey, and Sydney.

Nobody said very much. Everybody was very, very tired. They just ate their shawarma quietly, and it was warm, and it was good.

Paisley leaned her head on Bruce's arm. Lainey fell asleep with a piece of bread still in her hand. Sydney drew a picture in her notebook of everyone sitting at the table together.

Steve looked down the table at all of them. Then he looked at Tim.

"Nobody got left behind," said Steve. "Your family made sure of that. You were captains today too."

Tim looked at Lindsey. She was smiling at him with tired, happy eyes. Then he looked at his three girls, all sleepy and dusty and proud.

"We had a good team," said Tim.

Tony took one last bite of his shawarma. "Huh," he said. "This is actually really good."

And for the first time ever, the Avengers all agreed on something.
